# Brand Naming Candidates

Working title: **GroceryManager** (ship under this until an owner decision is made).

These three candidates were selected for App Store safety, pronounceability, distinctiveness in the
Food & Drink category, and compatibility with a calm, minimal design system.

---

## Candidate 1 — Pantri

**Pronunciation:** PAN-tree (identical to "pantry")

**Tagline:** Your pantry, on autopilot.

### Logo direction
Wordmark-led. Lowercase `pantri` in a rounded-grotesque face (weight: medium). Icon: a stylised
shelf bracket or arch shape — one clean curve suggesting a pantry alcove — in garden green with a
warm off-white fill. Works at 16 px favicon scale. No food illustrations; the shape is architectural,
not illustrative. Dark-mode variant swaps fill to near-black.

### Voice / tone
Helpful and matter-of-fact, like a competent flatmate who keeps the kitchen organised without making
a fuss. Sentences are short; microcopy is active ("Scanned 3 new items", "You're running low on
oats"). Warm but never gushing — no exclamation marks in UI strings, light use in marketing.

### Why this name wins
"Pantry" is among the highest-intent search terms in the grocery-app category (verify in App Store
Connect Search Ads before committing) and Pantri claims it with a modern, app-native spelling that
is immediately understood by any English speaker.

### Why this name might not win
The -i respelling reads as a trend-following convention (Fiverr, Tumblr) that can feel dated in a
few years; competitors could crowd the "pantry" keyword cluster without infringing the mark.

## Candidate 2 — Mise

**Pronunciation:** MEEZ (from French "mise en place")

**Tagline:** Everything in its place.

### Logo direction
Pure wordmark: lowercase `mise` in a thin-weight geometric sans (Hanken Grotesk Light or similar).
No icon on the splash screen — the name is the mark. On the app icon, a single horizontal line
bisects a circle, evoking a kitchen counter or a chopping board seen from above. Palette: deep
forest green on linen white; accent is the same garden green used throughout the app. Feels like a
Parisian cookware brand.

### Voice / tone
Quietly confident and a little editorial, borrowing from the design-forward food publication world.
Copy is precise — never vague, never breathless. The brand trusts users to be curious cooks, not
beginners who need hand-holding. Example taglines: "Mise knows what you have. Mise knows what you
need." A warm authorial "we" is used sparingly.

### Why this name wins
Culinary insiders immediately recognise the term; newcomers find it intriguing and Googleable,
giving the brand an organic search story and instant credibility with the food-literate audience
who will evangelise it.

### Why this name might not win
Non-cooking audiences may mispronounce it ("MIZ", "MY-see") creating word-of-mouth friction, and
the French origin requires consistent spelling discipline across every touchpoint.

---

## Candidate 3 — Larder

**Pronunciation:** LAR-der (rhymes with "harder")

**Tagline:** Know your kitchen.

### Logo direction
Wordmark with a small ligature detail between `l` and `a` — a subtle nod to hand-lettering without
going fully script. Icon: a rounded arch (the classic larder door shape) with a tiny shelf line
inside — two lines, no more. Warm terracotta or aged-oak colour sits alongside the app's garden
green as a secondary accent, evoking a heritage kitchen rather than a tech product. Dark-mode
variant uses a warm near-black background with cream text.

### Voice / tone
Literary and grounding, with a sense of accumulated domestic wisdom. Copy reads like a trusted
cookbook author who has cooked for families for decades. Not precious or nostalgic — still
contemporary and direct — but with a warmth that makes the app feel like a kitchen companion rather
than a productivity tool. Avoids tech jargon entirely in consumer-facing surfaces.

### Why this name wins
"Larder" is distinctive, ownable, and trademark-clear in the App Store category; the heritage
register sets it apart from the sea of -ify/-ly/-r app names and signals that the product is about
the whole kitchen relationship, not just a list.

### Why this name might not win
"Larder" is unfamiliar to Gen-Z users in North America (more common in British/Australian English)
and may require explanation, increasing the educational burden in paid acquisition copy.

---

## Decision framework

| Criterion | Pantri | Mise | Larder |
|---|---|---|---|
| Instant clarity (cold audience) | High | Medium | Low–Medium |
| Culinary credibility | Medium | Very high | High |
| App Store keyword advantage | High | Medium | Low |
| Trademark distinctiveness | Medium | High | Very high |
| Works in 1 word aloud | Yes | Yes (if coached) | Yes |
| Longevity / trend risk | Medium | Low | Very low |

**Recommended default pick:** Pantri — lowest acquisition friction, highest searchability.
**Recommended if audience skews food-literate:** Mise — the strongest brand story.
**Recommended if longevity / premium positioning is priority:** Larder.
